# Point-Early / Topic Placement

**Genres:** ALL — universal craft, not genre-scoped
**Rescoped:** 2026-08-07 (June: "Williams style craft should be for ALL writing." Originally tagged `gra-memory-creation` because it was written during that genre build; topic placement is a general craft move.)
**Category:** register / pre-draft tilt
**Added:** 2026-07-04 (gra-memory-creation genre build, T2)

## The Move

Put the what-this-is where the reader looks for it: the front of the sentence, the front of the
paragraph, the front of the field. Don't make a reader travel through scene-setting before learning
what they're reading.

This folds into the skill's existing what-this-is-opener rule (`gra-metabolize` §1e) — same
requirement, named here as a Williams craft move (topic placement) so it has a place in the pre-draft
moves alongside the other three.

## Why It Works — It Kills the Chronology Opener Before It's Written

The R4 v1 failure (`METABOLISM_LOG.md`, the BRIEF's original corpus) opened with session
chronology — "By Session 2, the metabolism process had..." — before ever saying what the record
actually was. A reader has to hold that unresolved scene-setting in mind until the point finally
arrives. Point-first prevents this at the sentence-planning stage: decide what the record's point is
*before* drafting the opening, and put it first.

## Should / Shouldn't

- **Shouldn't (chronology-first):** "By Session 2, after several rounds of revision, the team had
  arrived at a distinction between correction and elaboration that mattered for how supersession
  chains work."
- **Should (point-first):** "Correction and elaboration are different relations, and conflating them
  breaks supersession chains — this record is the distinction, reached over Session 2's revision
  rounds."

## Relationship to the What-This-Is Opener

The what-this-is opener (mandated in the skill) must survive the same test this move implies: does
it do real orienting work, or is it itself throat-clearing dressed as an opener? See
`throat_clearing.md` for the deletion test that catches an opener that fails this.
