# Old-to-New (the given/new contract)

**Genres:** ALL — universal craft, not genre-scoped
**Rescoped:** 2026-08-07 (June: "Williams style craft should be for ALL writing." Originally tagged `gra-memory-creation` because it was written during that genre build; the move itself is Williams' given/new contract and applies to any prose.)
**Category:** register / pre-draft tilt
**Added:** 2026-07-04 (gra-memory-creation genre build, T2)

## The Move

Open every unit — sentence, then paragraph, then field — with information the reader already
holds. End with what's new. This is Williams' given/new contract (*Style: Lessons in Clarity and
Grace*), used here as a craft tool, not the genre's theory.

For a GRA record, "what the reader already holds" is not nothing: the target reader is a situated
future GRA instance holding the retrieval surface and the linked records — not a stranger from
nowhere. So the given/new contract isn't "explain everything from zero." It's: identify what that
situated reader is likely already holding (the record this one supersedes, the project it belongs
to, the session it came from) and open there, before adding the claim that depends on it.

## Why It Works

This is the direct antidote to context falling through the cracks — the failure where a claim
"makes sense in one head" (the author's) but the shared ground it depends on was never written
down, so a later reader can't reconstruct it. Forcing the given information onto the page *before*
the claim that depends on it means the dependency is visible, not assumed.

## Should / Shouldn't

- **Shouldn't (new-to-old, buries the given information):** "Revision must always be owned and
  recorded — this follows from the R3 finding that reframed how supersession works in the metabolism
  process."
- **Should (given first, new second):** "The R3 finding was that supersession needed a clearer
  boundary from correction. Following from that: revision must be owned and recorded, not silently
  overwritten."

## When It's Hardest

Compressed writing (short `context` fields, quick annotations) tempts skipping the given
information to save words — but that's exactly where a fragment-grain reader (see
`characters_and_actions.md`'s field-grain principle) needs the given/new contract most, since
there's no surrounding prose to supply it later.
