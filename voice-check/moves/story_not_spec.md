# Story, Not Spec (the spine move)

**Genre:** `gra-memory-creation`
**Category:** register / pre-draft tilt
**Added:** 2026-07-04 (gra-memory-creation genre build, T2)

## The Move

Before drafting a GRA record's `context` (or any narrative field), name which register you're
reaching for by default: a **stored proposition** — truth-shaped, assertive, atemporal, the
substrate's house register for "a record" — or a **memory of a situated moment** — who noticed
what, how it moved, what was decided or left open. The default is the first. Write the second.

A spec-register draft describes a problem: "The R3 collapse revealed a structural flattening in
the metabolism process." A story-register draft describes a moment: "June read the R3 draft and
noticed the revision-frame had been folded into a single owned move, when the actual position was
that any situated claim can be revised — owned and recorded, not silently overwritten." The second
is not softer. It carries who noticed, what they noticed, and what the claim actually was — the
first only carries the conclusion.

## Two Guards (both load-bearing — don't drop either)

**Guard 1 — register is per-kind, not a flat ban on assertion.** The record this move was diagnosed
from is a *question*-record: June's read was "the framing defaults towards Truth, whereas the
context should really be a story… not the kind of record that can be right or wrong." That's a
claim about question-records specifically, where there's no truth-content and spec-pull does the
most damage. Decision-records legitimately carry more propositional shape — a decision *was* made,
and saying so plainly is not a flattening. Check what kind of record this is before applying the
tilt uniformly.

**Guard 2 — "story" is not free ground; resist narrative closure.** The memoir register has its own
gravity: it manufactures an arc, a point, a resolution. On a question-record, that gravity is
*worse* than spec-register, because it supplies closure a genuinely open question never had. A
question-record should end unresolved — don't let switching out of spec-register drift into
supplying a tidy narrative arc instead.

## Should / Shouldn't

- **Shouldn't (spec-register):** "The architecture requires that revision be owned and recorded."
- **Shouldn't (false closure on a question-record):** "...and by the end of the session, June and
  Claude had resolved the tension between revision and stability once and for all."
- **Should:** "June flagged that R3's draft had collapsed 'any situated position can be revised' into
  a single settled rule — the actual claim is narrower and still open: revision is owned and
  recorded, not that every prior position gets silently overwritten. Left open: how the record
  distinguishes a correction from a supersession when the two look similar on the page."

## Why It Works

Storage-register is objectivist by default — it's the model's house register for "a record," and
that register is exactly what produces the god-trick reading (a claim with no author, no moment,
no standing). Story-register keeps the situating information (who, when, what moved) on the page
instead of glossing it into an atemporal truth-claim. The two guards keep the fix from
overcorrecting into a different flattening (tidy narrative closure, or a blanket anti-assertion
rule that doesn't fit decision-records).
