# Secondary Contingent Study

**Genres:** `ea_grant`, `humanist_fellowship`
**Category:** scope management / reviewer positioning
**Added:** 2026-04-17 (SFF iChange arc session)

## The Move

Flag a secondary line of inquiry that would extend the core work but depends on external collaboration, infrastructure, or tool maturity the grant itself isn't funding. Use the explicit format:

> **Secondary [type of study] (contingent on collaboration).**

Describe what the study would do, why it would strengthen the core claim, and then explicitly name the contingencies (collaborator availability, tool maturity, infrastructure access). State that the grant would not cover the infrastructure the secondary study depends on.

## When to Use

Use this move when ALL of these are true:

1. You have an opportunity to extend the core study in a way that would significantly strengthen positioning for a specific reviewer audience (bridging disciplines, addressing a reviewer's specific values).
2. The extension depends on external resources the grant shouldn't fund (collaborator availability, proprietary tool access, infrastructure outside the applicant's control).
3. You can state the extension's value without overcommitting to deliver it.

Do NOT use this move when:
- The secondary study IS the core study (then make it the core).
- The contingencies are within your control (then just commit to it).
- You're tempted to include every possible extension (limit to one; more dilutes the core).

## Example (SFF iChange arc, April 2026)

**Core study:** iChange deployment of Autograder and Reframe across school pilot sites, with behavioral research on the output-format bias mechanism.

**Secondary contingent study:** KV-cache geometric analysis applied to the same output-format mechanism — moves the architectural claim from behavioral evidence to mechanistic-interpretability evidence. Flagged as contingent on collaboration with the technique's developers; no SFF funds would go to external tool infrastructure.

**Why it worked:** Expanded reviewer reach across the three SFF Fairness Track recommenders:
- Kasirzadeh (structural injustice) — served by the core behavioral finding
- Solaiman (practical bias, industry) — served by iChange deployment
- Gyevnar (bridging AI Safety/AI Ethics) — served by the secondary mechanistic study

One sentence of contingency language preserved honesty. The Secondary Study didn't displace the core story, it added an angle of evidence the behavioral finding alone couldn't supply.

## Why It Works

- **Expands potential impact** without overcommitting.
- **Positions the applicant as networked** into the relevant field.
- **Answers reviewer values the core study doesn't fully address** — you can reach a multi-reviewer panel without distorting the core proposal.
- **Honest contingency framing** reads as more credible than ambiguous scoping. Reviewers trust applicants who name what depends on external conditions.
- **Bridge claim** ("produces a rare bridge between X and Y") positions the work as distinctive — a claim the core study alone typically can't make.

## Anti-pattern

Do not use this move to hide deliverables you're unsure about inside "contingent" framing. The contingency must be real — collaborator availability, tool maturity, infrastructure access that the grant isn't funding. Fake contingencies read as hedging.

## Template

> **Secondary [study type] (contingent on collaboration).** We flag a secondary line of inquiry that would use [technique/method] — [brief description of technique, grounded in existing research where possible] — to test whether [core finding] has a [dimension] signature. [One sentence of existing-research context]. Applied to our finding, the technique would let us ask: [specific research question]. If yes, [claim] moves from [current evidence type] to [stronger evidence type]. We flag this as secondary rather than core because it depends on [specific external contingency], which [grant fund] would not cover. If it lands within the grant period, it produces [distinctive framing of the bridge/contribution].
