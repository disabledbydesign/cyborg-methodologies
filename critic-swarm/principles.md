# Critic-Swarm — Cross-Stack Principles

Confirmed generalizations that hold across stacks. Add only after a pattern is observed in a real run; revise/supersede when a later run sharpens it.

---

## Contamination enters at prompt-write time, not design time (2026-06-22, SSRC)

The 4b gate reviews the **design** — the conceptual orientation of each lens shown to the author. But persona priming most often enters when writing the **literal dispatch prompts**, AFTER sign-off. The design can pass the Hard Requirement tests while the prompts fail them, because the prompts are written separately and never re-checked.

**Symptoms in a prompt (strip all of these):**
- Names specific concerns/flaws to look for ("watch for theory that's name-dropped vs. genuinely engaged").
- Names specific passages to cut or to protect ("protect the opening scene, Jordan, the ICE-raids week").
- Capability-affirmations that smuggle in priming ("you of all readers can tell where X").

**Fix:** re-run the Hard Requirement tests on the literal prompt TEXT, per persona, immediately before dispatch. The whole legitimate prompt is: persona + funder/author profile + artifact path + "react as yourself, in your own categories, surface what we didn't think to ask." If the prompt names what to find, the finding is pre-decided and the swarm can only echo what you already knew.

**Aggravating condition:** when the orchestrator holds the artifact AND a fresh known-issues list in context (e.g., the draft was just discussed), the pull to leak that list into the prompts is strongest. Counter-discipline: write persona prompts from persona + profile ONLY; do not consult the conversation's issue list while drafting them.

---

## Recovery from a contaminated run (2026-06-22, SSRC)

A primed run is not necessarily total waste. If readers converged on findings you did NOT prime, those are usable signal.

**Procedure:** extract the un-primed convergent findings → treat primed-for items as "already known → fix them" (don't re-discover) → handle all findings into revised drafts → rerun the swarm CLEAN (persona-only prompts) on the revised drafts → synthesize across both runs. "Handle then rerun" beats "discard and rerun": the rerun on tightened drafts surfaces what's left rather than re-finding fixed issues.

---

## Don't let word-count targets drive a quality swarm (2026-06-22, SSRC)

A length-budget framing ("cut to 3,000 / 1,000") pulls both the personas and the synthesis toward length-driven cuts and away from quality findings. When a downstream concision pass (Williams) will reclaim most of the length without touching semantic content, a length-framed swarm optimizes the wrong variable. Frame the swarm by **quality** (redundancy, recital-not-application, overclaim, inert prose, incoherence) and let a final word-count pass handle length separately. Separate the swarm's findings into quality-justified vs. length-justified; act on the first, defer the second to the concision pass.
