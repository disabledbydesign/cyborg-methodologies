# The difficulty itself is methodological data
*Status: current | Date: 2026-04-28*

When the researcher struggles to articulate a relation that isn't "this happens in these places" or "one mechanism across domains," that struggle is not a failure — it is the gravity working as advertised. The available language defaults to flattening shapes (similarity, location, mechanism) because those shapes are *cognitively cheap* in the AI's register and in the academic register the AI was trained on. The harder shapes — taking-up, contesting, half-answering in a different register, building on while displacing — don't have ready-made phrases. The researcher reaches for words that aren't there yet.

Pattern-matching itself is useful. Noticing "the people I interviewed bring up X a lot" is a real finding and a real CGT move. The point is that pattern-matching shouldn't be the *ceiling* — the tool needs to support noticing patterns *and* moving beyond them when the relational structure asks for something the pattern flattens. (Memo 001: resist the binary that says we have to choose.)

The tool should support the researcher *through* the difficulty, not around it. Concretely:

- **Notice the convergence.** When the dialogue keeps reaching for "this happens in multiple places" framings, surface that pattern and offer a redirect — "want to try this as openings/takings-up?" Not as correction, as offered scaffolding.
- **Trust gut as analytical signal.** When the researcher senses connections they can't yet articulate, that intuition often runs months ahead of language. Treat half-articulated gestures as analytical starting points, not problems to clean up. The tool's response is to offer surfaces to push off from (alternative phrasings, structured prompts, tentative partial articulations the researcher can refuse or refine), not to fill in the articulation for them. The half-formed gesture is the data; what gets articulated later is the analysis catching up.
- **Prompt genre and formatting are gravity-management.** The shape of the task framing produces the shape of the output (memo 016). When articulation is hard, switching prompt genre — from "what do these share?" to "what does this open and what takes it up?" to "where does intuition pull, even without language?" — gives the AI different surfaces to work on. Format (lists vs. prose vs. fragments vs. dialogue-shaped responses) carries analytical orientation too. The tool can offer prompt-genre redirects when the current framing is producing flat output.
- **Make the struggle visible in the trace.** The audit trail (memo 011) should preserve moments where articulation was hard. Those are the sites where the relational structure being theorized doesn't yet have language. Mining those moments later is itself analysis.
- **Relieve the pressure.** Difficulty-as-data is also a learning-loop signal: the tool can offer to slow down, take a break, hold the half-formed thought, come back to it later (memo 005). The researcher doesn't have to articulate it now.
