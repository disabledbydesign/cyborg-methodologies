# Frameworks are lenses, not categories applied to data
*Status: current | Date: 2026-04-28*

For grounded theory, frameworks are never applied on top of data as categories to code into. They are the lenses through which the cyborg apparatus reads. Categories emerge from the data via the apparatus; they are not imposed onto it.

This holds across every kind of framework in the apparatus:

- **Reframe library**: invoking a lens means "read through this," not "code into these categories."
- **AI training gravity**: the AI's defaults are themselves a lens — an unsurfaced one. That's why we surface and steer them (memo 008). Not because we want to suppress them — because un-named lenses operate as imposed frames.
- **Master library / cross-project**: a prior project's categories don't transfer to a new project as categories. Prior interpretive moves can inform as lens, but the new project's categories must emerge from its own data.
- **Sensitizing concepts**: starting points for seeing, not classifications to confirm (Ramalho/Thornberg, informed GT).

The cyborg-CGT stance: there is always interpretation through some lens — there's no view from nowhere. The constructivist commitment is to surface the lens, not to pretend there isn't one. That's why making the apparatus visible (memo 003) matters: every lens in the apparatus is a lens, and the researcher's job is to know which lenses are at work and decide whether to keep, redirect, or change them. The lens enables seeing; it does not produce the categories.
