# Quality criteria as build parameters, and the force relations behind them
*Status: current | Date: 2026-04-28*

Charmaz & Thornberg's (2020) four criteria — credibility, originality, resonance, usefulness — are the strongest available foundation, and we use them as build parameters: the tool surfaces them as reflective prompts at decision points (formalize? keep coding?), lenses for the researcher's judgment rather than a checklist. They function as ground truth — but ground truths are themselves constructed and revisable. They were articulated for human-only CGT practice; cyborg-CGT may surface places where they need refinement, extension, or reformulation. We work with them, watch where they don't quite fit, and revise as the practice teaches us.

The cyborg extension: does the practice produce theory neither human nor AI alone could produce? That's the criterion that sits alongside Charmaz & Thornberg's and asks the cyborg-specific question.

The deeper question, behind "what counts as good": *what force relations shape how AI codes and interprets data?* Training data composition, dominant frameworks over-represented in that data, scientism register baked into academic corpora, compression bias from summarization training, the political economy of which voices the model has read more of and which less — these are not neutral defaults. They are forces that quietly direct the assumptions orienting coding strategies. Algorithmic bias from training data is not a separate concern from quality criteria; it is a force relation that shapes what the AI takes "good coding" to look like before any researcher's judgment enters.

The cyborg-CGT response is not to pretend the forces aren't there, and not to try to neutralize them (there's no view from nowhere). It's to make the forces visible and steerable — through the lens-not-application stance (memo 009), through gravity-routing (memos 008, 010), through compression-resistance (memo 014), through reflexivity that extends to the apparatus (memo 003), through framework invocations that the researcher chooses rather than inherits. Quality, in cyborg-CGT, includes interrogating which force relations are doing the orienting — and that interrogation is not separate from credibility, originality, resonance, and usefulness; it is part of how those criteria get met under cyborg conditions.

Refusing agreement metrics (inter-coder reliability, agreement-with-human-coding) follows from this: those metrics assume one correct reading to agree on, which is itself a force relation — a positivist orientation embedded as quality criterion. CGT's stance is that readings are constructed; the cyborg practice extends that to include the AI's reading as one more constructed reading, in dialogue, in tension, shaped by force relations that are themselves part of what the analysis must surface.
