# Theoretical coding asymmetry: AI does not do this autonomously
*Status: current | Date: 2026-04-28*

A structural commitment, not a tunable parameter: AI does not perform theoretical coding in solo mode. There is no architectural path in this system that produces theoretical-level analysis without dialogic engagement with the researcher. This is a constraint on the build, not a configuration choice.

Why this is structural rather than a soft preference:

- **Theoretical coding is relationship construction across categories** — specifying how categories relate, what theory the relations construct, what the relations explain. It is exactly where compression bias (014), pattern-matching gravity (010), early-abstraction gravity (010), and coherence-smoothing gravity (010, 020) most damage the analysis. JMIR's empirical finding — "AI flattens nuanced connections at higher coding levels" — is largely theoretical-coding flattening. The pattern is consistent across the literature.
- **The creative leap (memo 012) is most needed here.** Theoretical coding is where the abductive move matters most — the introduction of new context, the unexpected relational reading, the shift in locus of scholarship. This is where dialogue is most load-bearing, where AI gravity is least useful, and where solo-AI output most reliably converges on shallow.
- **The cyborg practice's distinctive value emerges most at this stage.** The whole point of cyborg-CGT vs. command-tool AI qual is that the dialogue produces theory neither could alone. Theoretical coding is where that bet pays off — or fails. Letting the AI do it solo is exactly the move that voids the practice.

What this means for architecture:

- **No batch mode for theoretical coding.** Quick-scan and batch-coding modes (memo 006) operate at initial coding levels only. They do not produce theoretical analysis. The architectural separation is hard, not soft: the prompts, workflows, and outputs at theoretical-coding tier are dialogic by structure.
- **No "theoretical synthesis" output type.** The system does not generate documents that purport to be theoretical analyses. It generates conversations within which theoretical analysis gets constructed dialogically. The distinction is in what the system *produces* — material to think with, not finished theoretical claims.
- **Initial and focused coding can have higher AI autonomy** (memo 002, where dialogue is load-bearing as empirical question). The asymmetry is real: AI brings useful capacity at initial coding (breadth, surfacing non-obvious codes, gerund discipline, in-vivo identification), less at focused, none-without-dialogue at theoretical. This is not a continuous gradient; the theoretical tier is categorically different.
- **Refusal as feature.** When the researcher asks the tool for theoretical-level output without dialogic engagement, the tool refuses — not as "I can't" but as "this isn't a thing the practice does. Want to start a theoretical coding conversation?" The refusal is itself methodological practice. (Resist binary collapse, memo 001: this isn't refusal-of-the-AI, it's refusal of a specific architectural shortcut. The AI is fully present at theoretical coding — *in dialogue*.)

Connection to other memos: this constraint is what makes scalable rigor (the original DESIGN_PLAN concept) work without sliding into "AI does CGT" claims at low rigor. Quick-scan is exploratory pattern-noticing; working analysis includes dialogic theoretical work; publication-quality includes deeper dialogic theoretical work and full reflexivity. None of the tiers includes "AI alone produces theoretical findings."
