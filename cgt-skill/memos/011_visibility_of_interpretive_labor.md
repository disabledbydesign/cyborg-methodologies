# Visibility of interpretive labor
*Status: current | Date: 2026-04-28*

Every coding move, every category shift, every memo decision leaves a trace the researcher can interrogate. The point isn't bookkeeping — constructivist GT requires the researcher to be able to *show their work* (Charmaz: "with constructivist GT you can't hide"). The trace lets the researcher see what shaped a category, where a divergence got resolved, when a lens got invoked, where AI gravity was redirected. The audit trail is part of the apparatus (memo 003) — it's how the apparatus stays steerable instead of opaque.

The trace is also generative: it's what the learning loops feed on (memos 004, 005). Mining the trace lets us double-check, synthesize, surface patterns across sessions, and see where the cyborg practice is doing its work vs. drifting. The audit trail enables analysis of the apparatus, not just compliance.

The tool produces the trace as a side effect of normal use, not as an extra step.
