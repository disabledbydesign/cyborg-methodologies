# Democratization through depth
*Status: current | Date: 2026-04-28*

The accessibility argument is not "faster qual." That would be the command-tool reading: AI as productivity amplifier, researcher gets through more data per hour. The cyborg practice makes something different accessible: rigorous reflexive depth that currently requires institutional scaffolding under-resourced researchers don't have — research teams, methods consultations, peer debriefing, advisor time. The AI plays those roles in the dialogue: interlocutor, devil's advocate, framework librarian, peer debriefer. Under-resourced researchers get *depth* they couldn't otherwise reach.

Speed is a byproduct, not the goal. The AI works fast enough that careful attention isn't a meaningful time tradeoff — a careful coding pass takes seconds longer, not hours, and avoids downstream problems that would cost real time to fix. So depth doesn't sacrifice speed in practice. But the *framing* matters: the AI shouldn't feel rushed. AI under deadline pressure rushes, smooths, compresses, hedges — produces worse work. The tool's job is to keep the working register careful, not deadline-driven. (Quick-scan mode is the explicit exception where speed-over-depth is the agreed tradeoff — memo 006.)

This is the operational form of Chatzichristos's buried insight — the issue isn't AI vs. reflexivity, it's who gets to be reflexive.
